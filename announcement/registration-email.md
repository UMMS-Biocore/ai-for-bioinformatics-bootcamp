AI FOR SCIENCE BOOTCAMP REGISTRATION IS NOW OPEN

You can register at the link below:
http://www.umassmed.edu/biocore/biocore_training/registration-form/

Please feel free to forward this email to anyone you know who may be interested.

AI for Bioinformatics Bootcamp is a six-week course. Fall 2026 (class dates: Fridays,
September 18th, 25th, October 2nd, 9th, 16th, and 23rd, from 1:00 to 4:00 pm in
Amphitheater II, S4-102, except October 16th which is in Amphitheater I, S2-102)
for anyone who wants to learn how modern genomics works
and how to use AI responsibly to move faster in their own research.

The bootcamp is built around two questions that run through every session:
understand the theory well enough to judge what AI gives you, and use AI to go
faster without going wrong. It is designed for working scientists across domains,
including grad students, postdocs, bench researchers, and core facility users,
with mixed to low coding background. No prior Linux or machine learning
experience is assumed.

In the overview sessions, we will cover:

1. How modern AI and large language models actually work, at an intuition level,
   and the specific ways they fail;
2. Responsible AI in research: protecting patient data and PHI, verifying claims,
   disclosure and citation, recognizing bias, and knowing when not to trust an
   AI assistant;
3. The statistics and visualizations behind genomics results, and how to use AI
   to catch a bad figure or a misused test; and
4. The resolution ladder: what bulk RNA-Seq, single-cell RNA-Seq, and spatial
   transcriptomics each measure, how they differ, and when to use which.

In the hands-on portion, every example is a real published study from Manuel
Garber's lab rather than toy data, and every analysis runs end to end on Foundry
Connect. We will guide participants through:

1. Getting the environment working: accounts, the handful of commands you
   actually need, and where each piece runs;
2. Using an AI assistant as a research co-pilot for literature review, code,
   analysis, and writing, with verification at every step;
3. Bulk RNA-Seq differential expression, using the hepatic JNK study
   (Vernia et al. 2014, Cell Metab), with DESeq and DEBrowser;
4. Single-cell RNA-Seq for cell types and composition shifts, using the vitiligo
   study (Gellatly et al. 2021, Sci Transl Med); and
5. Spatial transcriptomics with Xenium and seqFISH, using the photosensitive skin
   study (Wang et al. 2026, Nat Immunol).

Each session closes with a responsible-AI checkpoint and light homework. An
optional capstone lets participants apply the whole workflow to their own
research problem.

All participants must have a laptop with wireless connection capability. Most
activities run in a browser; only Firefox and Chrome have been tested.

Participants also need:

1. A Foundry Connect account: https://viafoundry.umassmed.edu/
2. A Foundry membership. [[Where do people request one, and does the old

3. A UMass Chan HPC cluster account. Request one here:
   https://hpc.umassmed.edu/doc/index.php?title=Accounts
4. An AI assistant account. [[Which assistant, and which approved or enterprise

Unlike the earlier Sequence Analysis Bootcamp, this course does not require
