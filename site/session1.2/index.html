Session 1.2 · Act one · Eric Ma, Moderna

How language models actually work.

One trick, next-token prediction, explained well enough that you can teach it to your own lab and trace any answer a model gives you back to it. No math, no code. Every demo runs a real, tiny model in your browser.

Open Eric's interactive lesson → Use the arrow keys to move between sections

What the lesson covers

  1. One trick. A language model is a very well-read autocomplete, predicting the next piece of text.
  2. Tokens. Text becomes numbers, which is why models miscount letters and garble exact strings.
  3. Dice and temperature. A probability list, then a weighted roll. Why answers vary between runs.
  4. Training. Where the numbers come from, and why the model copies flaws in its training text.
  5. The big library. It interpolates within what it read. It does not look things up, and it stops at a cutoff date.
  6. Confidently wrong. Hallucination is the mechanism working normally. Quiz: spot the invented references.
  7. Scale. What changes as models grow, and what does not.
  8. Foundation models. The same machinery for proteins, genes, and cells: AlphaFold 2, Geneformer, scGPT, Enformer.
  9. Limits. Where the trick breaks, and the habit that catches each failure.
