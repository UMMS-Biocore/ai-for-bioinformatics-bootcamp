Session 3.3 · Eric Ma, Moderna

AI as a research copilot.

John’s knowledge base is read by an agent. Eric’s goes one step further: it is used inside the analysis, side by side with the data. One enzyme, four kinds of data, and one question, worked through live, so you can see where each kind of evidence helps, where it misleads, and how an AI copilot keeps every claim traceable.

Open Eric’s demo → Seven notebooks with code and results · Nothing to install to read them

The question

Where do activity-improving mutations of the enzyme IRED-88 come from, and could we have seen them coming by combining DMS data, structure, prediction, and literature?

IRED-88 is an imine reductase from an enzyme engineering campaign Eric worked on at Novartis, published in ACS Catalysis in 2021. The campaign measured 4,720 single mutants, the enantioselectivity of 310 engineered variants, and a crystal structure. Next to it sits a six-paper knowledge base in the format you built in 3.2.

The question ladder

  1. Which mutations improve activity? The deep mutational scan shows which single mutations help, and where they sit along the sequence.
  2. Are the hits where the structure says they should be? Each position’s distance to the NADP cofactor in the crystal structure.
  3. What does prior work already know? The top hits, checked against the knowledge base.
  4. What does prediction see that the crystal cannot? ESMFold fills in the residues the crystal leaves out, with a confidence score attached.
  5. Additive or epistatic? Doubles and triples of the best mutations test whether a claim from the literature survives the data.
  6. Would we have noticed a hole? Hide part of the scan where the literature matters, and see what the rest of the evidence recovers.

Read every notebook, with its code and results.

Four kinds of data, four jobs

DataWhat it is good at
Deep mutational scanDiscovery: finding hits nobody guessed
Crystal structureMechanism: ruling out bad guesses
Knowledge baseExplanation: naming what worked, and why
Structure predictionBlind spots: reaching what the crystal misses, with a confidence score
