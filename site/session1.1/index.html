Why this bootcamp exists

This is the successor to the Sequence Analysis Bootcamp. The old course spent its first weeks teaching you to produce code. That part got cheap.

“Producing the code is cheap. Understanding the code is more expensive now.”

Eric Ma

Today in three acts

Think of AI as an aircraft. First you learn how the plane works, then how an experienced pilot flies it, then the flight rules and the airport you will fly from. By the end of the afternoon you can explain why a language model is sometimes confidently wrong, and you know the rules and habits that keep that from reaching your results. Times are approximate.

Keep this checklist open all afternoon

Nine questions to ask before any AI output enters your work. Each speaker today answers some of them. At the end (1.4) we fill in the rest, and it becomes your homework.

  1. Could this be a fluent guess rather than a fact?Answered by Eric (1.2)
  2. Would I get the same answer if I asked again?Eric (1.2) and Tommy (1.3)
  3. Could this be out of date?Eric (1.2)
  4. Did I check it against the primary source or the raw data?Eric (1.2) and Tommy (1.3)
  5. Do I know enough to notice if it is wrong?Tommy (1.3)
  6. Is there anything here that should not leave our walls?Alper (1.4)
  7. What was the AI allowed to do, and what did it do?Tommy (1.3) and Alper (1.4)
  8. Could someone else trace how this result was made?Tommy (1.3) and Alper (1.4)
  9. Would I be comfortable disclosing exactly how I used AI here?Alper (1.4)

Ground rules and what to expect

In the room

  • No patient or unpublished data in any AI tool today. Everything we show uses public data or our own test runs.
  • Every session is recorded. Materials and links live on these pages and in the GitHub repository.
  • Laptops open, but you do not need to type along. The live demos are for watching. Hands-on work starts with the domain sessions.
  • Questions are welcome. Ask during or at the end of each part.
