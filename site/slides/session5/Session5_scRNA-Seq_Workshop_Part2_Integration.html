Part 3
Integration
Remove what differs between patients for technical reasons, keep what differs for biological ones, and check that it worked.
What integration should remove, and what it should keep
Remove
  • Library-to-library differences: depth, capture, ambient RNA, handling
  • Patient background that isn’t the question
  • Goal: the same cell type from different patients overlaps
Keep
  • Cell types and states as separate groups
  • Populations found in one condition only: inflammatory macrophages, neutrophils in severe disease
  • Rare or patient-specific biology: plasma cells in C152
The batch key tells the method what to remove. Use the library or patient, never the condition you want to compare.

Don’t integrate on the condition
It removes the answer
The batch key says “these differences are noise”. With condition as the key, the condition effect is what gets removed.
It misses the real batch
Each patient is its own library. Correcting by condition leaves patient-to-patient differences in place.
It can’t be checked
Once condition differences are corrected away, nothing tells you whether they were real.
Even correcting by patient has a catch here: every patient belongs to one condition, so mixing patients also pulls condition-specific cells toward each other. That is why we check the boundaries afterwards.

What UMAP distorts
  • Distances between clusters
  • Cluster sizes and densities
  • Whether groups touch or separate
  • It changes with the random seed and settings
So in this workshop
  • Clusters come from the graph, not the picture
  • Integration is judged by neighbor statistics
  • Nothing is computed or tested on UMAP coordinates

Resolution is a choice

Markers here are descriptive

Part 5
What changes between conditions
Which cell types gain or lose ground, and which genes change within a cell type, with patients as the replicates.
Two kinds of change
Composition
Which cell types gain or lose ground?
  • Share of each cell type, per patient
  • Compare patients across groups, not pooled cells
  • Shares add up to 100%: one type rising pushes the others down
Expression
What changes inside a cell type?
  • One cell type at a time
  • Sum counts per patient (pseudobulk), then test
  • Uses raw counts, not the integrated embedding
Bulk RNA-seq mixes these two; single-cell data lets us separate them.
