Session 4.1 · Manuel Garber, UMass Chan

Why differential expression uses models.

DESeq2, edgeR, and limma do not run a t-test on every gene. Four short notebooks show why: how expression measurements are distributed, how a t-test is already a linear model, what changes when the data are counts, and why a gene measured in three replicates needs to borrow information from all the others.

Open the notebooks → Fri Oct 9, 1:00 to 4:00 pm

The session in one sentence

The shape of one gene’s histogram is a weak reason to use a count model. The strong reasons are the mean–variance relationship, sharing information across genes, and the design matrix.

The four notebooks

Run each with uvx marimo edit --sandbox <file> from the notebook folder, Documents/bootcamp/session4/bootcamp-bulk-rnaseq-main. In a new terminal, go back to it first. Every code cell has a short explanation above it.

How are expression measurements distributed? 01_distributions.py

GAPDH and ACTB on microarrays look roughly normal. The same genes in RNA-seq come out as raw counts clumped by sequencing depth, until you normalize for depth. Stable genes at lower expression show where a normal distribution stops fitting the counts, below about 10 reads. The notebook ends with mean against variance within replicates.

The t-test is a linear model 02_ttest_is_lm.py

A two-sample t-test and the model y ~ group give the same t statistic and p-value. Writing it as a model pays off as soon as the experiment has structure: adding a batch term separates the treatment effect from batch, and in the simulation the batch model detects an effect the plain t-test almost always misses.

Count data and GLMs 03_counts_and_glm.py

The same simulated biology measured three ways: qPCR, microarray, and RNA-seq. Biological variation between samples plus Poisson sampling of reads gives the negative binomial distribution. A negative binomial GLM is then compared with ordinary regression on log counts, gene by gene and across many genes for false positives and power.

Estimating each gene’s variance 04_variance_estimation.py

With three replicates, a gene’s variance estimate is so noisy that its 95% range spans about 150-fold. Replicate-against-replicate scatters show a constant band for arrays and a funnel for RNA-seq, where Poisson noise dominates at low counts. Mean expression explains most of the gene-to-gene variation in RNA-seq variance, so a curve fitted across all genes, and shrinkage toward it (the idea behind limma’s eBayes and DESeq2’s dispersion prior), gives better estimates, checked against held-out donors.

The RNA-seq panels in notebook 1 read count tables that are not public, so Manuel shows them live. Everything else runs on your computer. Notebook 4 needs BULK_RNASEQ_DIR set, as in 4.0.
