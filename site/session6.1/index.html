A few words on spatial data

Manuel Garber opens the session by placing spatial data next to what you already know. A spatial experiment is a single-cell table with a position for every cell, so its analysis builds on the single-cell steps from Session 5. What position adds is proximity: which cells actually sit next to each other.

Part 1: why spatial

Bulk sequencing is a smoothie, single cell gives each fruit, and spatial adds a table of where every cell sits. The analysis pools all samples, clusters and labels cell types as in Session 5, then puts the labels back on the tissue. Manuel then turns to what this adds: ligand and receptor analysis in single-cell data can only guess which cell types talk to each other. With spatial data you can follow a cell type across the skin, look at the cells right next to it, and see which of them express the signal.

Part 2: reading the plots, and choosing a technology

A question on the proximity plots: cells are grouped by position across the dermis, and for each group the plot averages a signal in the neighboring cells. Then the technologies. Xenium and seqFISH are imaging methods. Xenium measures ten times more genes, but the number of transcripts per cell is similar, and that is what lets small groups of cells be compared. Sequencing-based methods capture spots on a grid, so a spot may hold part of a cell or several cells.

What the lecture covers

  1. The shape of the data. Bulk RNA-seq gives one column of read counts per sample. Single-cell gives one column per cell within each sample. Spatial keeps that per cell table and adds an X and a Y for every cell.
  2. It builds on single-cell analysis. Quality control, normalization, clustering and naming cell types work as in 5.1. Spatial analysis starts from there.
  3. Why spatial. Inferring communication between cells from ligand and receptor expression, or even from their downstream targets, is far from optimal when you do not know whether the two cells ever meet.
  4. Spatial can include proximity. Position lets you ask which cells are neighbors, and so whether a sender and a receiver are close enough to talk.
  5. Not all technologies work the same way. Spatial methods differ in how they read RNA in place, and that shapes what the data can show. 6.2 compares imaging and sequencing based methods.
