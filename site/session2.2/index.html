Session 2.2 · Ming “Tommy” Tang, AstraZeneca

Master six plots and you can make most figures.

Six plot types carry about ninety percent of the figures in a genomics paper. This part walks through each one: what it shows, when to reach for it, how to read someone else's, and the ways each can mislead you.

The six

PlotWhat it shows
BarplotA value per category. The one most often used where a distribution belongs.
Scatter plotTwo measurements per point, and the relationship between them.
Line graphSomething measured along an ordered axis, usually time or dose.
HistogramThe shape of one distribution, including the tails a summary statistic hides.
Boxplot and violinDistributions side by side across groups.
HeatmapA matrix of values, with clustering and annotations on the margins.

Also in this part

  • What the fancy plots are underneath. A clustered dot plot, a volcano, a UMAP panel: each is one of the six with extra encoding layered on.
  • Two figures read live. One from a published paper and one from a recent Nature study, taken apart to see what the authors chose and what they left out.
  • How a plot misleads. Bars hiding a distribution, axes that do not start at zero, and overplotting that turns thousands of points into a solid blob.
